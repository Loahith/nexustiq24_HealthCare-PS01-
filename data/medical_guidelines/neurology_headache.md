# Guideline: Evaluation of Acute Headache & Neurological Symptoms

Source: Adapted general intake triage reference (non-diagnostic, for staff guidance only).

- Sudden, severe "thunderclap" headache reaching peak intensity within seconds to minutes is a critical emergency red flag suspicious for subarachnoid hemorrhage.
- Acute focal neurological deficits such as facial droop, arm weakness, slurred speech, acute confusion, or loss of balance warrant immediate emergency escalation for potential stroke.
- Severe migraines accompanied by nausea, photophobia, phonophobia, or visual aura without focal deficits should be classified as urgent and routed to Neurology.
- Gradual-onset, mild tension headaches without red-flag symptoms can be categorized as standard priority.
- Always check for fever, stiff neck, altered mental status, or sudden onset.
