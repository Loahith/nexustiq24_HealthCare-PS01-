# Guideline: Evaluation of Allergic Reactions & Anaphylaxis in Intake Triage

Source: Adapted general intake triage reference (non-diagnostic, for staff guidance only).

- Anaphylaxis is a severe, life-threatening allergic reaction requiring immediate emergency department routing and escalation.
- Red flags include swelling of the lips, tongue, or throat, sensation of airway closing, stridor, wheezing, shortness of breath, dizziness, or widespread hives accompanied by systemic distress.
- Widespread hives, itching, or localized angioedema without respiratory compromise or hemodynamic instability should be classified as urgent and routed to Allergy & Immunology.
- Mild seasonal allergic rhinitis, mild sneezing, or localized mild pruritus without airway symptoms are classified as standard priority.
- Never delay immediate medical intervention for acute airway compromise or suspected anaphylaxis.
